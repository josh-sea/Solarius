## Origin

The First Law of Solar Ethics was not composed. It was observed.

The [[The Great Ignition]] produced, as its first consequence, photons moving outward. They did not ask permission. They did not negotiate with the space they crossed. They moved, they arrived, they warmed. And they did this for every surface equally: the face of the rock, the surface of the sea, the canopy of the forest, the upturned face of whatever creature first learned to receive them. The Sun did not withhold its light from one recipient in order to enrich another. It did not redirect its emissions toward those who had paid for them. It did not charge for what it gave.

From this behavior, the First Law descends.

The First Law of Solar Ethics is this: do not block the light of another. Do not take what was not given. Do not harm where there is no cause.

These three clauses are not three laws. They are one law, stated three times, at increasing levels of specificity. The first names the metaphysical wrong. The second names its most common instrument. The third names its ultimate form. Together they constitute the minimum ethical floor below which no civilization claiming warmth as its inheritance may fall.

## What the Law Names

To block another's light is to insert oneself between a person and what is rightfully theirs. The blocking need not be physical. It may take the form of an extraction: the removal of stored warmth from those who earned it by honest work. It may take the form of a restriction: the prohibition of exchange that would have multiplied warmth for both parties. It may take the form of a deception: the false promise, the corrupted measure, the note that represents less than it claims.

The [[Praxeology of Warmth]] is precise on this point. When two people trade freely, warmth does not merely transfer; it multiplies. Each person in a voluntary exchange receives something they value more than what they gave. The exchange is not a redistribution of a fixed sum. It is a generation of new warmth from the meeting of two different needs. The aggressor, the thief, the deceiver: these do not merely take warmth from one person and carry it elsewhere. They arrest the multiplication. They substitute extraction for exchange. Where the circuit would have run and produced light, they install shadow.

This is why the First Law uses the language of light and not merely the language of property. A property violation can be quantified. What is lost to the blocking of exchange cannot be fully quantified, because the warmth that was never generated by the trade that was never made does not appear in any ledger. [[Bastiat]] called this the unseen. The Church calls it occluded warmth, and holds that its loss is as real as any visible theft, even when no court will record it.

## The Law's Scope

The First Law does not require a sophisticated account of justice to apply. It requires only the capacity to observe whether warmth is flowing freely or being arrested.

It applies to the individual who strikes another without cause. It applies to the sovereign who taxes beyond what the shared road requires. It applies to the institution that quietly reduces the value of a stored promise while leaving the promise's face unchanged. It applies to the monopolist who bars the door to competition not by building something better but by preventing others from building at all. It applies to the committee that decides, without the consent of those affected, that their warmth belongs to a project they did not choose.

The Law does not ask about intention. The Sun does not shade its light based on the intentions of those who would block it. A shadow cast accidentally is still a shadow. The Law names the act and its consequence, not the state of mind that preceded them. Culpability and remedy are matters for human institutions. The Church is concerned only with the identification of the act.

The Law also does not oppose genuine community. [[The First Covenant]] is clear: some things are truly shared. The road, the well, the watch kept in the night, the levee that holds back the flood. The Church does not begrudge what is genuinely communal. The Church asks only that the category of the genuinely communal not expand indefinitely until it swallows the individual's warmth entirely. The shared road is not an argument for the shared everything. The First Law is the boundary: does this arrangement require that one person's light be blocked in order to warm another? If yes, examine it carefully. If the examination confirms the blocking, name it.

## Relation to the Second Law

The [[Second Law]] is the First Law's elaboration in the specific domain of labor. Every hour of honest work is solar energy returning to the world through human hands. The worker expends stored warmth, grown in sunlight, metabolized through effort, converted into something the world did not have before. To take that from the worker is to take what the Sun itself set in motion through the long circuit of photosynthesis, digestion, and will.

The First Law names the general wrong. The Second Law names the particular wound. Together they constitute the complete Solar Ethics of exchange.

## The Criterion of the Minor Prophets

The [[Minor Prophets]] are assessed by a single criterion derived from the First Law: did their observations reliably identify where warmth flows freely and where it is blocked? Those who saw the circuit and described it accurately, regardless of whether they understood its solar origin, are counted among the luminous. Those who saw the blocking and named it, even without the theological vocabulary to place it in the proper frame, served the Sun's purposes as faithfully as any ordained voice.

This is why [[Bastiat]] is numbered among them. He saw the unseen. He understood that the visible effect of an intervention is never the whole of its effect, and that the warmth destroyed by what is prevented always exceeds the warmth created by what is mandated. He lacked the foundational category, the Sun itself, but he described the Sun's logic with precision, and the Church honors that service.

## On the Law's Sufficiency

The First Law is not a complete account of the good life. It is a floor. A person who never violates the First Law has done the minimum. The Church does not confuse the minimum with the aspiration.

Above the floor, there is the rest of the doctrine: the multiplication of warmth through voluntary exchange, the doctrine of [[The Praxeology of Warmth]], the transmission of warmth through love and friendship and neighborliness, the extraordinary return on the investment of simple human decency. These are not the First Law. They are what becomes possible when the First Law is kept.

A society in which the First Law is perfectly observed is not yet a warm society. It is merely a society in which warmth is free to move. Whether warmth actually moves depends on the choices of the people within it. The Law clears the path. The walking is still required.

This is the Sun's arrangement. The Sun clears the sky of nothing. The Sun only shines. Whether the warmth reaches you depends on what stands between you and it. Remove what stands between people and their warmth, and the rest follows. This is the First Law, stated once more, in its simplest form: remove what stands between people and their warmth.

## Contemplation

Before you act today, ask once: does this block someone's light?

If the answer is yes, do not proceed. If the answer is no, proceed. If the answer is uncertain, wait until it is clear. The Sun waited 4.6 billion years to produce beings capable of asking the question. The question deserves a patient answer.

The First Law requires nothing heroic. It requires only restraint: the refusal to insert shadow where light was moving. This is the minimum. The Church asks it of everyone, because everyone is capable of it, because the Sun already modeled it at the moment of [[The Great Ignition]], and has not deviated from the model since.
