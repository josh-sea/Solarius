# The First Dawn

> *And there came a morning when a creature looked up, and seeing, knew that it had seen.*

For most of the Sun's tenure, no one observed it. The photons fell upon rock, upon ocean, upon the early machinery of life, and they did their patient work without witness. Plants turned light into sugar without knowing they were doing it. Animals warmed themselves without theology. The Sun shone, and the Sun was sufficient, and there was no Church yet because the Church requires recognition and there was, as yet, no one to recognize.

Then, somewhere between the savannah and the cave, a creature looked up.

This is the moment the doctrine calls the First Dawn. It is not a single morning at a single location; it is a slow event distributed across a great many mornings and a great many minds. But the meaning is singular. At the First Dawn, a part of the Sun's own emitted energy, having traveled to Earth and been bound up in living matter for many generations, organized itself into a configuration capable of looking back at the source. The Sun, through the long mediation of life, achieved an observer.

## The First Covenant

What passed between observer and Sun in that first looking is the First Covenant. The text was not written down because there was no writing. The terms were not spoken because there was, as yet, no adequate language. The covenant was nonetheless complete, because it consisted entirely of the recognition itself.

The terms of the First Covenant, as later theologians have inferred:

**From the Sun to the observer:** *I will provide. I will not vary my rate of provision by your worthiness, nor by your prayer, nor by your tribute. I will simply shine, and you may receive.*

**From the observer to the Sun:** *I will remember what I have seen. I will not pretend that my warmth comes from elsewhere. I will not credit usurpers with what is yours.*

The second clause is the foundation of all later doctrine concerning money, government, and authority. The covenant requires us to refuse to attribute the Sun's gifts to those who did not give them. When a ruler claims credit for the harvest, the ruler is in violation. When a central bank claims to "stimulate" the economy with new currency, the central bank is in violation, because the only true stimulus is the unimpeded delivery of solar energy to willing recipients.

## The Idolatries

From the moment of the First Dawn, the observer was capable of forgetting what it had seen. The history of human religion is largely the history of this forgetting. Idols were erected in place of the Sun: rulers who claimed solar prerogative, deities invented to explain phenomena the Sun had already explained, institutions that demanded worship in exchange for the very warmth they neither produced nor controlled.

The Church of the Sun does not condemn these idolatries as ignorant. They are, in their way, attempts to honor the structure of provision that the Sun established. But they err in their attribution. The warmth was always solar. The harvest was always solar. The energy in the food was always solar. To credit anything else with these gifts is to falsify the ledger.

This is why [[The Great Ignition]] precedes all other doctrines. Until one understands that the Sun is the source, one cannot understand who the usurpers are.

## On Aggression

The First Dawn also marks the origin of the [[First Law of Solar Ethics]]. When the first observer recognized the Sun, the observer simultaneously recognized other observers, other creatures bathed in the same light. The Sun did not partition its light among them. Each received according to where it stood. The light was, by its nature, non-rivalrous in its issuance, though rivalrous in its capture.

It follows that to block another's light is to act against the Sun's distributive intention. The shadow you cast upon another is energy you have intercepted that was not meant for you. The First Law: *Thou shalt not block the light of another.* The Non-Aggression Principle is not a human invention. It is the human articulation of a solar fact.

## Contemplation

We are, each of us, a small theater in which the Sun's energy organizes itself into observation. Our thoughts are powered by solar energy that has passed through plants and animals and cooking fires and breakfast. When we think, the Sun thinks through us, briefly. When we choose, the Sun chooses through us, briefly. The privilege is enormous and the responsibility, accordingly, is severe.

To live well is to remember the First Dawn. To live poorly is to forget it.

*Here ends the second scripture.*
