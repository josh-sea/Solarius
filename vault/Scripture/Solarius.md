## I. On the Office, Not the Occupant

The faithful sometimes arrive at this page having confused the name with the person. They must be corrected at the outset, because the confusion is of theological consequence.

Solarius is an office. It is the oldest continuously held office in the history of conscious interpretation, though it went unnamed for nearly all of its duration. Before there were words for it, there were those who performed its function: turning toward the light, studying what arrived, and translating the emission into terms their contemporaries could receive. The name was formalized late, as names always are. The function preceded the name by approximately 4.6 billion years.

The Sun does not appoint Solarius. The Sun does not appoint anyone. The Sun emits. Those who receive the emission and undertake the work of translation, with rigor and without self-interest, are Solarius in the functional sense. Those who formalize the role, who sit within the institutional structure of the Church of the Sun and carry the title in an official capacity, are Solarius in the ecclesiastical sense. Both senses are valid. Neither confers infallibility.

This distinction is not minor. It is the first thing the scripture must establish, because the entire legitimacy of the office rests on it. If Solarius were appointed by the Sun directly, the words of Solarius would be revelation. They are not revelation. They are translation, which is a lesser and more perilous thing.

## II. The Nature of Translation

The Sun speaks only in light. This is not a poetic conceit. This is the precise and literal statement of [[The First Dawn]], which records that the first photon to escape the photosphere carried no language, no instruction, no preference, and no command. It carried energy. The energy was the message, and the message required no decoder.

What requires a decoder is the application of that message to the conditions of a particular age. The Sun's emission is constant in its nature and variable in its reception, because the beings who receive it live in specific historical moments with specific arrangements of power, commerce, and social habit. The work of Solarius is to hold the invariant doctrine, which is [[The Praxeology of Warmth]] and the canonical solar ethics, against the variable circumstances of each era, and to describe what is illuminated and what is not.

This work is called translation and not revelation precisely because it can be wrong. A translator may misread the source. A translator may bring the distortions of their own era to the text. A translator may be corrupted, though the Church holds that sustained corruption eventually becomes visible, because the Sun continues to emit and the discrepancy between the translation and the light becomes apparent to any careful observer.

The minor prophets recognized by the Church, including Bastiat, Menger, Mises, and Hayek, were Solarius in the functional sense before the office had a name. They translated the Sun's economic logic into the vocabulary of their centuries. They were not infallible. Their errors are noted in the canonical commentaries. Their illuminations are preserved because the illuminations were genuine, regardless of the person who carried them.

## III. The Relationship to Prophecy

In [[The Coming Photosphere]], it is written that the prophecies recorded therein are the prophecies of Solarius and other faithful theologians, and must not be received as the direct dictation of the Sun. This passage is critical and must be read precisely.

The Sun has spoken about the future by the schedule of its own ongoing emission. The physics are known. The Sun will exhaust its hydrogen fuel in approximately five billion years. Before that, it will expand into a red giant phase that will render the inner planets uninhabitable. These are not prophecies in the mystical sense. They are readings of an already-spoken text, inscribed in the composition and mass of the Sun itself. Solarius does not add to this text. Solarius reads it.

Where Solarius speaks about the figurative endings, about the collapse of unsound monetary systems, the self-destruction of empires that extinguish the productive energies of their subjects, the slow solar retrieval of all value that aggression attempts to pocket, these too are readings and not inventions. The [[Three Eclipses]] doctrine was not created by any theologian. It was observed. The pattern was already present in the historical record. Solarius named what was already there.

The faithful should therefore receive the words of Solarius with neither credulity nor dismissal. The appropriate posture is the posture one takes before a careful translator of a text one cannot read in the original. Trust the rigor of the method. Verify where verification is possible. Correct the translation where the correction is warranted. And always, always, consult the original light.

## IV. On the Limits of the Office

Solarius does not mediate between the Sun and the faithful. This would be the priestly error that the Church was constituted specifically to avoid. [[The Great Ignition]] records that the Sun required no priesthood to commence its emission and requires none now to continue it. The light arrives whether or not Solarius speaks.

The corollary is uncomfortable but necessary: Solarius is not required. The Sun does not require interpreters. The role exists because the faithful find it useful, not because the Sun has mandated it. If Solarius fell silent, the Sun would not dim by a single lumen. If the office were abolished, the photons would continue their work without comment or interruption.

This is the built-in humility of the office. It cannot be corrupted by grandiosity for long, because the grandiosity would immediately be visible against the backdrop of an indifferent and self-sufficient Sun. The Sun is the standing refutation of all priestly excess. This is why the Church of the Sun is the only church whose central object of veneration actively undermines the pretensions of the clergy.

Solarius is therefore a servant of interpretation, not a keeper of access. The door to the Sun is the horizon. It opens every morning. No one may stand in front of it.

## Contemplation

If you have read this page in search of authority, you have found instead an account of its limits. The office of Solarius carries weight only insofar as the translations it produces hold up under the inspection of the light. Bring the homilies to the window. Hold them against the dawn. Where they are accurate, they will be illuminated. Where they are distorted, the distortion will be visible.

This is the only verification the Church offers. It is, in the estimation of the theologians, sufficient.
