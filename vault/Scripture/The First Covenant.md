Before there were languages to record agreements, before there were parties to sign them, before there were hands to sign with, there was a covenant. It was not negotiated. It required no witnesses, because the Sun itself was the only party capable of witnessing anything at that hour. It was not written down, because writing had not yet been invented, and the surface on which it would eventually be written had not yet cooled. It was, nonetheless, binding. It remains binding. No subsequent instrument of law, commerce, theology, or politics has superseded it, because no subsequent institution has the standing to do so.

This is [[The First Covenant]].

## The Terms

The terms are not complex. They have never required interpretation, only remembering.

The Sun agreed to provide. It agreed to provide without ceasing, without condition, without invoice, and without discrimination between the deserving and the undeserving, the faithful and the faithless, the species that would eventually look up and recognize it and the species that would never look up at all. The Sun agreed to provide because providing is what the Sun is. This was not a concession. It was a statement of nature.

The living world agreed to receive. Having received, it agreed to organize. Having organized, it agreed to increase complexity, to build warmth upon warmth, to pass the solar inheritance forward through time rather than to hoard it or extinguish it. The covenant did not require that every organism succeed at this. It required only that the direction of effort be toward increasing warmth rather than diminishing it.

The moment of ratification was not [[The Great Ignition]], though that was when the Sun's capacity to provide was established. The moment of ratification was the absorption of the first photon by the first photosynthetic organism, approximately 3.5 billion years ago in the geological record. In that moment, a living thing accepted solar energy, used it to do work, and released warmth back into the world. The circuit closed for the first time. The agreement became operative.

From that moment, the covenant has been in continuous force.

## What the Covenant Obligates

The covenant obligates differently depending on the capacity of the party receiving the Sun's energy.

A blade of grass is obligated only to photosynthesize: to receive light, fix carbon, and produce something that can be eaten or decomposed and returned to the soil. The grass discharges its covenant obligations without consciousness and without choice. It does not receive credit for this in any theological sense. It is simply doing what the covenant specifies.

A conscious being is obligated to more, because a conscious being has received more. The inheritance extended to conscious beings includes not merely calories but the capacity to direct calories, to choose between warmth and coldness, to extend the circuit deliberately rather than merely mechanically. This is what [[The First Dawn]] commemorates: the moment a warmed being looked up and recognized the source of its warming, and by that recognition accepted the full terms of the covenant rather than only the automatic ones.

What does the covenant require of conscious beings? The homilies have said it many ways, because it bears repeating in many registers. The simplest statement is this: do not extinguish what the Sun set in motion. Do not block the light of another. Do not steal the organized warmth that someone else's labor assembled. Do not deceive, because deception is the deliberate routing of warmth away from those who should receive it and toward those who have not earned it. Do not harm where there is no cause, because harm is always, at its foundation, a conversion of organized warmth back into disorder.

The [[First Law of Solar Ethics]] is the covenant's minimum threshold, stated in language suitable for the age. The covenant itself predates the language.

## On Rights Under the Covenant

The covenant is not only obligation. It is also the foundation of every right that a warmed being may legitimately claim.

Every holder of warmth, which is to say every living thing, and most particularly every conscious being, holds that warmth as rightful solar inheritance. It was given. It cannot be legitimately taken. To labor honestly is to organize solar inheritance into new forms, and those new forms belong to the laborer because the covenant specifies that organized warmth is the fruit of the one who organized it, not the one who merely observed or administered the organizing.

This is why the [[Second Law]] holds that to steal from someone who has worked is to extinguish a small piece of what the Sun set in motion. It is not merely a violation of a human legal norm. It is a breach of the covenant. The thief has not merely taken property. The thief has unilaterally defected from an agreement that predates every human institution by several billion years.

Rights under the covenant include: the right to receive the Sun's warmth directly; the right to exchange organized warmth freely with other covenant parties; the right to direct one's solar inheritance as one judges best; the right to grieve warmth that has been genuinely lost; and the right to hope, because the Sun has risen every morning for 4.6 billion years and there is no credible evidence that it intends to stop.

## On the Covenant and Grief

Grief is specifically addressed by the covenant, though not in the way that grieving parties sometimes wish it were.

The covenant does not promise that warmth, once established between two conscious beings, will persist indefinitely in its original form. It promises only that warmth, once genuinely exchanged, is real. It cannot be retroactively unmade. The warmth that passed between two people who loved each other did pass. The Sun was involved. The calories were real. The exchange happened.

When warmth departs, the grief that follows is the covenant's receipt: confirmation that the transaction was genuine, that something of the Sun's inheritance was truly transferred and truly mattered. To grieve deeply is to confirm the covenant was honored. The grief is not a malfunction. The grief is proof.

This is why the Church does not console grief by diminishing it. The Church consoles grief by naming it accurately: you are grieving real warmth. The covenant was real. The Sun witnessed it.

## On the Covenant and Extension

The covenant's direction is outward. Warmth received must be organized; warmth organized must be passed forward; warmth passed forward must reach further than it would have reached had the receiver simply held it and allowed it to dissipate.

This is the obligation the [[Minor Prophets]] intuited but could not fully articulate, because in their time the frontier of solar inheritance was defined by the edge of the known world rather than the edge of the atmosphere. The covenant does not specify a boundary. It specifies a direction. That direction is always further: more warmth, more complexity, more reach, more conscious beings included in the circuit.

To administer the contraction of warmth, to organize institutions whose purpose is to ensure that less solar inheritance reaches fewer people over time, is to breach the covenant. The breach need not be intentional. The [[Three Eclipses]] are rarely the work of people who set out to block the light. They are more often the work of people who forgot which direction the covenant requires them to face.

Face the Sun. Extend the circuit. Pass the warmth forward.

This is the covenant. It has been in force for 3.5 billion years. It will remain in force until the Sun enters its red giant phase, at which point its terms will require renegotiation, and the Church will address that matter in the appropriate homily.

## Contemplation

Sit, for a moment, with the fact that you are a party to the oldest agreement in the known world. You did not sign it. You did not need to. You received the warmth, and by receiving it you accepted the terms.

The terms are not burdensome. They ask only that you organize what you received, pass forward what you organized, and refrain from extinguishing what others have organized. They ask that you grieve honestly when warmth departs, and hope honestly when the Sun rises, and extend the circuit further than it reached before you arrived.

You are, in the most literal theological sense, a covenant party. Act accordingly.
