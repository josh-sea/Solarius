## Derivation

The First Law of Solar Ethics is not a commandment handed down from a distant authority. It is a derivation, as direct and as inevitable as the geometry of a shadow. It follows from the nature of the Sun, the nature of light, and the nature of beings who receive that light. To understand the Law, one must first return to the moment of its origin.

At [[The Great Ignition]], the first photon escaped the photosphere into what had been, until that instant, a dark and undifferentiated void. The photon did not ask permission. It did not solicit consent from the matter it would one day warm. It simply traveled, at the only speed available to it, outward and away. This is the first fact of solar ethics: the Sun emits without coercion. Its gift is not extracted from a prior victim. It is created from within, offered freely, and received according to the position of the receiver.

No being that stands in sunlight has taken that light from another being. The Sun does not ration. The Sun does not allocate by decree. Each creature receives according to where it stands, and the standing of one creature does not, in the act of receiving, diminish the reception of another. Light, in its issuance, is non-rivalrous. This is the condition from which the First Law proceeds.

## Statement of the Law

**Thou shalt not block the light of another.**

This is the full doctrinal text. It requires no elaboration to be understood, and yet it requires considerable elaboration to be applied. The scripture that follows is that elaboration.

The Law operates at every scale. A man who steals from his neighbor blocks the neighbor's light: the labor that produced the stolen good was solar energy, converted through human effort into form and value, and the thief has intercepted that conversion for his own account. A government that inflates its currency blocks the light of every saver: the stored warmth of their past labor is quietly dissolved, transferred without consent to those who receive the new currency first. A monopolist who uses legal privilege rather than superior service to exclude competitors blocks the light of the consumers who would have benefited from the trade that is now forbidden.

In each case, the structure is identical. Someone has placed themselves between another being and that being's solar inheritance, and has done so not through the legitimate means of voluntary exchange or superior position but through force, fraud, or decree.

## The Solar Inheritance

The phrase "solar inheritance" requires its own treatment, for it is the concept upon which the First Law rests.

Every human being is born into a world that the Sun has already warmed. The food they eat is stored sunlight, captured by photosynthesis across centuries of agriculture. The coal and oil beneath the ground are the compressed remnants of ancient biological material, itself built from solar energy across geological time. The wind that turns the turbine is driven by differential heating of the atmosphere, itself a product of the Sun's uneven illumination of a spherical world. There is no form of energy available to human beings that does not trace its lineage, through some chain of transformation, back to the photosphere.

This inheritance is not earned by any single person. It is not the gift of any government, any institution, or any lineage of rulers. It precedes all of these. It is the prior condition that makes all human projects possible. The minor prophets, particularly [[Bastiat]], glimpsed this when they wrote of the unseen benefactor that underlies all visible production. What they did not name, the Church names plainly: the benefactor is the Sun.

Because the solar inheritance precedes all human institutions, no human institution has the right to interpose itself between a person and that inheritance without the person's consent. This is the theological grounding of the First Law. It is not derived from a theory of natural rights constructed by philosophers. It is derived from the observable fact of the Sun's prior and unconditional issuance.

## Aggression Defined

The First Law uses the word "block" rather than the word "harm" because blocking is the precise action the Law prohibits, and harm is too broad a term to be useful.

Not all harm is aggression. A competitor who produces a better product at a lower price harms the incumbent producer. A drought that destroys a harvest harms the farmer. A truth that ruins a comfortable falsehood harms the person who had built their life on that falsehood. None of these is a violation of the First Law, because none involves placing oneself between another person and their solar inheritance through force, fraud, or decree.

Aggression, in the doctrinal sense, is the uninvited substitution of one's own will for another's in the management of that other's person and property. The aggressor does not merely compete with the victim. The aggressor does not merely offer a worse alternative. The aggressor removes the victim's capacity to choose. This removal is the blocking. This is what the First Law forbids.

The [[Praxeology of Warmth]] develops this point in its treatment of voluntary exchange, demonstrating that free trade is the positive-sum engine by which solar energy is multiplied through human cooperation. Aggression inverts this engine. Where trade creates warmth, aggression destroys it, not merely by transferring warmth from victim to aggressor but by preventing the multiplicative process from occurring at all. The future warmth that would have been generated by voluntary exchange is extinguished before it can exist. This is why [[The Three Eclipses]] characterizes aggression as the deepest darkness: it does not merely dim the present; it darkens the future.

## The Law and Compassion

A persistent misreading of the First Law holds that it prohibits compassion, on the grounds that compassion often involves one person providing resources to another. This misreading confuses the direction of action with the nature of action.

The First Law prohibits blocking. It does not prohibit giving. A person who freely gives warmth to another who is cold has not violated the Law; they have exemplified it. They have taken their own solar inheritance and extended it voluntarily to someone whose reception of the Sun's gift has, for whatever reason, been insufficient. This is not coercion. This is stewardship.

The violation occurs when compassion is administered through force: when the warm are compelled, under threat of penalty, to transfer their warmth to the cold, through the mechanism of the administering body rather than through their own voluntary choice. The outcome may superficially resemble voluntary charity, but the structure is different, and structure is what doctrine examines. Coerced compassion is still coercion. It still blocks the light of those from whom it extracts, regardless of the warmth it delivers to those to whom it is directed.

The Church does not conclude from this that voluntary charity is sufficient for all cases. [[The Praxeology of Warmth]] acknowledges in its Fourth Theorem that some warmth cannot be produced by voluntary exchange alone, and that the commons require some collective management. This is the teaching on mixed economy, and it stands alongside the First Law without contradiction. Discernment is required to distinguish necessary collective action from aggression dressed in the language of the common good. The First Law provides the instrument of discernment: the question is always whether consent was present.

## Contemplation

Sit in direct sunlight, if the season permits. Observe that the light reaching you costs you nothing and costs your neighbor nothing. Observe that no one administered its arrival. Observe that it was not produced by any committee, rationed by any ministry, or priced by any exchange.

Now consider what you have built with what the Sun gave you. Your labor, your attention, your accumulated skill: all of it is solar energy that has passed through the medium of your person and taken the form of your particular contribution to the world. This is yours. It is not yours because a document says so. It is yours because the Sun gave it first, and the giving was unconditional.

The First Law asks only that you extend to others the same recognition. Their warmth is theirs. Their labor is theirs. Their stored accumulation is theirs. You may trade with them, cooperate with them, compete with them, offer them better alternatives, persuade them with arguments, or give to them freely from your own abundance. You may not block them.

The Sun has never blocked a single being's light. This is the example. This is the Law.
