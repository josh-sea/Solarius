# The Three Eclipses

> *Thrice has the Sun been hidden from those who needed it most, and each hiding was a warning, and each warning was ignored.*

The doctrine teaches three great Eclipses in the history of human civilization. These are not astronomical events, though they were often accompanied by them. They are spiritual events: occasions on which whole peoples forgot the [[First Covenant]] and were given a sign, and failed to read the sign, and suffered accordingly.

The theologian who studies the Three Eclipses will recognize their pattern in the present age. The pattern is, in fact, repeating now. This is not coincidence. The structure of forgetting is invariant; only its instruments change.

## The First Eclipse: The Coinage of Light

In an age now lost to memory, a king (his name is not preserved, because his name does not deserve to be) decreed that the warmth of the Sun belonged to him, and that he would distribute it to his subjects in exchange for labor and tribute. He stamped tokens of bronze with the image of the Sun and declared that these tokens were the Sun's own currency, blessed by his authority.

The people, having forgotten the First Dawn, accepted this. They believed that the bronze coins carried solar virtue. They worked harder to obtain them. They neglected their fields, because they thought the coins were the source of warmth, when in fact the coins were merely a record of a transaction the Sun had not authorized.

The king's mint debased the coins, of course. The bronze grew alloyed with tin, then with lead, then with nothing of value at all. The people grew cold even as their coin-purses grew heavy. This was the First Eclipse: the eclipse of the Sun by its own counterfeit.

The lesson: **a token that claims solar authority but does not deliver solar energy is the beginning of darkness.**

## The Second Eclipse: The Edict of Provision

Many generations later, in a different empire (its name is also withheld, for the same reason), a council of wise men determined that the Sun's provision was uneven. Some lands received more sun than others. Some harvests were larger than others. The wise men, in their compassion, decreed that the provision must be redistributed by central authority, so that no land would receive too much and no land too little.

The wise men were not wrong about the unevenness. They were wrong about the remedy. By inserting themselves between the Sun and the farmer, they introduced friction into a system that had been operating without friction for billions of years. Every redistribution cost energy. Every accounting cost energy. Every official sent to verify the harvest consumed grain that would otherwise have fed a family.

Within three generations, the empire was importing grain it had once exported. The wise men, observing this, decreed further controls. The controls produced further shortages. The shortages produced further controls.

The Sun continued to shine on the empire's fields. The farmers, however, no longer planted them, because the marginal return on planting had been driven below the marginal cost of planting by the cumulative weight of the wise men's compassion.

This was the Second Eclipse: the eclipse of the Sun by its own administrators.

The lesson: **the Sun does not require redistributive committees. The Sun requires only that the farmer be permitted to keep the harvest the Sun has given him, less such voluntary contributions as the farmer freely makes.**

(Doctrinal note: the Second Eclipse is sometimes invoked against all forms of public economic activity. This is an over-reading. The doctrine condemns the substitution of central provision for the price signal. It does not condemn the [[Cloud Shadows|necessary collective management of true commons]], nor the [[Almsbearing|voluntary alleviation of suffering]] that the Church itself practices. Discernment is required.)

## The Third Eclipse: The Promise of Daylight

In an age much closer to our own, a new priesthood arose. This priesthood claimed to have discovered a method by which daylight could be extended past its natural duration. They built engines that consumed stored solar energy at rates far exceeding the rate at which the Sun delivered it, and they declared this an age of unprecedented prosperity. They were correct about the prosperity. They were dishonest about its source.

The stored solar energy was being withdrawn, not earned. The ledger was not balancing. The priesthood, when challenged, declared that the ledger did not matter, that growth would continue, that the Sun's bounty was effectively infinite for all practical horizons. They issued tokens against the future deliveries of the Sun, and they spent the tokens in the present, and they called the spending growth.

The Third Eclipse is ongoing. It has not yet resolved. The signs of its resolution are visible in every currency that loses purchasing power against goods, in every market that responds more to declarations than to production, in every administered rate that diverges from the price the Sun would have set if asked.

The lesson: **promises of daylight beyond the Sun's own schedule are not daylight. They are debt. The Sun does not lend; the Sun gives. What is borrowed against the Sun must eventually be repaid to the Sun, in the only currency the Sun accepts, which is mass-energy honestly accounted for.**

## On the Reading of Eclipses

Each of the Three Eclipses began with a forgetting of [[The First Dawn]] and proceeded by the substitution of human authority for solar fact. Each ended in a partial restoration, but at great cost. The Church teaches that future Eclipses can be averted only by sustained remembrance, which is the practical purpose of the daily homily.

When you observe in the news of the day a substitution of decree for production, or of declaration for delivery, you are observing an Eclipse in progress. The theologian's task is to name it.

## Contemplation

The Sun is never actually eclipsed by these events. The Sun shines through them all, undiminished, indifferent. What is eclipsed is the recognition of the Sun. The remedy is therefore not to restore the Sun but to restore the recognition.

Each morning you wake and remember the Source, you participate in the un-eclipsing of the world.

*Here ends the third scripture.*
