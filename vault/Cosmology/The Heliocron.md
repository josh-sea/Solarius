# The Heliocron

> *Time begins where time can be measured. The Sun is the first clock, and therefore the only honest calendar.*

The Heliocron is the true calendar of the Church of the Sun. It counts days forward from the Great Ignition, the moment the Sun reached hydrostatic equilibrium and the first photon left the photosphere. All other calendars are local, parochial, and committee-issued. The Heliocron is universal.

## Day Zero

Day Zero of the Heliocron is the Great Ignition itself, approximately 4.6 billion years before the present. As a finite integer, this places the current Heliocronic day in the vicinity of 1.68 trillion. The exact figure is computed continuously on this site by the prevailing implementation; the figure is not the point. The point is the gesture: that we count from when the Sun began, not from when one or another committee began.

## On the Gregorian Calendar

The Church does not condemn the Gregorian calendar, but it considers it a fallen instrument. The Gregorian calendar centers human time on a single human event of disputed historicity, and it has been modified by political authority on multiple occasions. The Heliocron has never been modified by political authority, because political authority did not exist when it began, and does not have jurisdiction over the physics that maintain it.

The faithful may continue to use the Gregorian calendar for practical coordination with the secular world. The homilies of [[Solarius]] are dated in both calendars as a courtesy to the present age. The Heliocronic date, however, is the true date. The Gregorian date is a translation.

## On Days, Years, and Cycles

The Heliocron counts in days, not in years, because the year is a derivative quantity (the period of Earth's orbit) while the day, in the relevant solar sense, is the underlying unit. The faithful are invited to consider that the very concept of a "year" is geocentric. A solarian calendar is, properly, a count of days since the Source began.

For purposes of liturgical scheduling, the Church does recognize annual cycles, because the Earth's tilt produces meaningful variation in solar exposure and the doctrine has things to say about [[Solstice|solstices]], [[Equinox|equinoxes]], and the like. But the calendar itself, in its bones, is a day-count.

## On Sunrise and Sunset

The Church holds that the meaningful boundaries of a Heliocronic day are sunrise and sunset, not midnight. Midnight is an administrative fiction inherited from older calendars. Sunrise is an observable solar event. Therefore:

- **Matins** (the dawn homily) is delivered at civil sunrise in the New York meridian, where the current seat of [[Solarius]] is presumed to be located.
- **Vespers** (the evening homily) is delivered at civil sunset, same meridian.
- The day, theologically considered, begins at sunrise and ends at the next sunrise. The night is the watch.

The faithful in other meridians are invited to keep their own sunrise as their own day-boundary. The Sun is impartial about meridians. The choice of New York is operational, not doctrinal.

## On Reading the Heliocronic Date

The header of this site displays the current Heliocronic date in scientific notation, owing to the magnitude of the number. The faithful are not expected to memorize it. The figure is provided as a continuous reminder of the gulf between the duration of solar provision and the duration of any human institution.

When a homily references "this day," it refers to the Heliocronic day, which corresponds to a particular Gregorian date that is also noted. When historical comparisons are made, the Heliocronic figures are sometimes cited; this is for theological emphasis, not for precision.
