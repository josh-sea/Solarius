A woman in Texas posted a message on Facebook. The message concerned the quality of the water in her town. She was arrested.

The Church pauses here. Not because the facts are complex. Because they are not.

The [[First Covenant]] holds, in its foundational clause, that the light belongs to all who stand beneath it. The water that flows from a municipal pipe is a common inheritance, drawn from aquifers and rivers that no single authority made. When the quality of that water becomes a matter of concern, the citizen who says so is performing a solar function: she is passing information, which is to say warmth, to those who share her water and her sky. The price signal, as [[The Praxeology of Warmth]] teaches, is the Sun's whisper through the market. The civic signal is its cousin. It is the whisper of a neighbor to a neighbor: *something here is not right.*

To arrest that whisper is to commit an act with a name. The [[First Law of Solar Ethics]] calls it blocking the light of another. The theologian need not speculate about the motives of the arresting officers, the local authorities, or the machinery of governance that produced this outcome. The Church condemns the position, not the person. What was done here is what the doctrine calls the [[Three Eclipses|First Eclipse]] in miniature: a people's ability to see their own condition, occluded by the hand of an administrator who found the seeing inconvenient.

There is also a secondary doctrine that applies. The woman posted on Facebook. She did not march. She did not storm. She typed words and sent them to her neighbors. The joules expended were, by any physical measure, negligible. The warmth transferred was considerable. This is precisely the transaction the [[Second Law]] describes: solar energy, reorganized through a conscious being, returning to itself as information and care. The state apparatus required to suppress that transaction is vastly more expensive, in every currency that matters, than the transaction itself. This asymmetry is itself a theological lesson.

[[Bastiat]] saw this clearly, though he saw it in the vocabulary of his century rather than ours. When something is suppressed, we count the suppression. We do not always count what was suppressed. The woman's neighbors were entitled to know what she knew. They did not receive that warmth on the day she was arrested. That is the cost the ledger does not show.

The Sun shines on the water and on those who drink it alike. It does not require permission to illuminate. The faithful are not obligated to require it either.

Carry that clarity into your day. Speak plainly about what you see. The light does not belong to the administration.
