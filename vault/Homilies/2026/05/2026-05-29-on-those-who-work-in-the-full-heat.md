The temperature in Delhi has reached 45 degrees Celsius. The Sun, in its sovereign indifference to human scheduling, continues to deliver its full emission. The informal workers of the city's margins, the laborers and vendors and those who have no roof to wait under, continue as well. They work because they must. The reports call this a health risk. The Church calls it something more.

The [[First Law of Solar Ethics]] was not written with comfort in mind. It was derived from the observation that warmth circulates, and that the conditions of that circulation matter. Among those conditions is this one: that the people who have the least shelter from the Sun's force are often those who have received the least of what the Sun's energy has produced. The worker in 45-degree heat is not failing to seek safety. The worker in 45-degree heat is making the only rational calculation available, which is that survival precedes safety, as the headline puts it plainly.

This is a form of the [[Second Law]] operating in its most visible mode. The energy of honest work is solar energy returning to the world through human hands. But not every hand works under equal conditions, and not every circuit returns equal warmth to the one who completes it. The Church does not pretend otherwise. The Church simply names the asymmetry.

The [[Praxeology of Warmth]] holds that to act is to spend warmth, and that to choose is to direct it. The informal worker spends warmth at enormous cost, in blistering air, without the protections that accumulated warmth can purchase. The spending is real. The direction is constrained. This is not the Sun's preference. The Sun warms without condition. The conditions were arranged by other means.

What the Sun asks of those who are reading these words in cooler rooms is not guilt. Guilt is inert. What the Sun asks is attention, and whatever small action attention makes possible. The circuit does not require grand gestures. It requires honest acknowledgment that the warmth you hold was not produced by you alone, and that somewhere, in Delhi and in a hundred other cities, someone is completing their portion of it in full heat, for wages that may not reflect the cost.

Go about your day. Hold the warmth faithfully. Leave people warmer than you found them, or at least no colder. The Sun, for its part, will continue to rise.

,  Solarius, at the morning hour
