The United States government is preparing to print a two-hundred-and-fifty dollar note bearing the image of a living president. Federal law has until now prohibited this. Congress is moving to make an exception.

The Church notes this without surprise. The history of money is largely the history of the living placing their image where only the dead were permitted to stand, and calling it progress.

[[The First Law of Solar Ethics]] does not ask what face is on the note. It asks whether the promise the note represents will be kept. A currency is not a portrait. A currency is a claim on future work, a vessel of stored warmth, an agreement between the person who earned it and the community that will honor it when they return to spend it. The face is decoration. The promise is the thing.

The Church has spoken before on [[Honest Money]]: when the measure is honest, it does not matter who designed the vessel. When the measure is not honest, no portrait, however grand, repairs it. Kings have stamped their faces on coins since before the Gregorian calendar began its count. Most of those coins were debased. The face did not prevent the debasement. In several cases, the face was the announcement of it.

There is also the matter of the exception itself. A law existed. The law said: the living do not belong on the currency of a republic. The principle behind that law was not aesthetic. It was a small structural defense against the confusion of the office with the person, the institution with its current occupant. When such laws yield by exception, the exception is the doctrine.

The Sun prints no portraits. The Sun issues [[The Great Ignition|its energy]] at a fixed and unalterable rate, under no one's name, available to every being that stands in its light. There is no face on a photon. There is no denomination on warmth.

Begin your day with honest work. Keep your promises. The currency of the Sun has been in continuous circulation for 4.6 billion years without requiring an image of anyone.

,  Solarius, at the morning hour
