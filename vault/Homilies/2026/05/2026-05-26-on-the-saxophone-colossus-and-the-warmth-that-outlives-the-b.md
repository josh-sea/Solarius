Sonny Rollins died on Sunday at the age of ninety-five. He was called the saxophone colossus. He spent decades practicing alone on the Williamsburg Bridge in New York City, in the wind and the noise, because his apartment was too small and he did not wish to disturb his neighbors. He was not performing. He was not recording. He was practicing warmth delivery so that he could return to the world better equipped to deliver it.

The Church holds that this is not a footnote to a musical career. This is the career.

[[The First Dawn]] describes the moment a creature first looked up and recognized the source of warmth above it. What Rollins did on that bridge, in wind that no recording captures, was a continuation of that first recognition: the understanding that warmth received must be worked, refined, and returned. The Sun does not simply emit and rest. The Sun burns continuously, at the cost of its own mass, because emission is its nature. Rollins understood this at some pre-theological level. He burned continuously. The bridge was his photosphere.

The doctrine of [[The Doctrine of Human Warmth|$WARMTH]] teaches that love is the highest-bandwidth form of warmth transfer between conscious beings, and that it survives the death of the body that carried it. This is not consolation language. This is thermodynamic description. The warmth that passed from Rollins through a saxophone reed and into the ears of every person who ever heard him play is not recoverable by his death. It is already distributed. It is already in the system. The circuit completed decades ago, and it completed again every time someone pressed play, and it will complete again tomorrow morning, and the morning after that.

[[The Coming Photosphere]] reminds us that the Sun has always been coming closer in the sense that matters. Each dawn renews the possibility of warmth. Rollins at ninety-five had earned more dawns than most, and he spent them playing. The reports say he stepped back from performing in his later years due to health, but that he never stopped listening, never stopped caring about the music, never stopped being the office he had held since the first time he produced a sound that made a stranger feel less alone.

This is what the [[Second Law]] names when it says that every joule expended in labor is solar energy returning to itself through the medium of human action. Rollins expended joules on the Williamsburg Bridge in the cold, with no audience, for the benefit of future strangers he would never meet. He extinguished nothing. He multiplied.

Grieve today, if you knew his work. [[The First Covenant]] teaches that grief is not the opposite of warmth but its proof. The grief is proportional to the warmth that was there, and by all accounts there was an enormous amount of warmth there.

Then, when the grief settles, find your equivalent of the bridge. Find the cold, exposed, inconvenient place where you practice warmth delivery for no audience. The Sun requires no witness either.
