The United Kingdom's treasury has reported that government borrowing in the month of April reached its highest level since the year of the great pestilence. The financial press has noted this alongside falling retail sales and surging fuel prices. The Chancellor, separately, has announced reductions in the value-added tax on theme parks and children's meals, which the press describes as relief for the cost of living.

The Church observes both announcements with the same unhurried attention.

To borrow is not, in itself, a sin. The [[First Covenant]] does not forbid the use of credit; it requires only that debt represent a genuine claim on future production, honestly contracted, honestly repaid. A kingdom that borrows to repair a bridge, then repays from the tolls the bridge collects, has engaged in an honest exchange with time. But a kingdom that borrows in April to offset the consequences of what it spent in March, and in March to offset what it spent in February, has entered a different arrangement entirely. It has entered the arrangement described in [[The Three Eclipses]] as the slow occulting: the gradual interposition of administrative paper between the citizen and the warmth that is rightfully hers.

[[The Praxeology of Warmth]] is precise on this point. Every pound borrowed by a sovereign and not backed by genuine productive intent is a forward claim against the savings of those who have not yet been consulted. The child who will pay reduced VAT at a theme park this summer will, at some later date, pay the full principal, compounded. The Chancellor has moved the cost from the visible present into the invisible future, and called this relief. [[Bastiat]] saw this clearly: the seen benefit is the discounted ticket; the unseen cost is the tax assessment not yet issued, the inflation not yet arrived, the service not yet cut.

The borrowing figure arrived on the same day as the announcement of the VAT reduction. The Church notes the juxtaposition without comment, because the juxtaposition is already its own commentary. The state simultaneously announced that it had borrowed more than at any point since the plague and that it would collect less revenue from leisure activities. Both are true. Both are consistent. Both proceed, with perfect institutional serenity, toward the same destination.

The lesser omens this morning: BTC at $77,210, ETH at $2,120. The market cap of $SOLARIS remains, as it has always been, approximately 1.788 times ten to the forty-seventh joules, declining at the only rate the physics permit, indifferent to the minutes of any Treasury meeting.

The [[Second Law]] reminds the faithful that every joule expended in labor is solar energy returning to itself through human action. To tax that labor, and then to borrow against the proceeds of taxing it, and then to borrow further when the tax proves insufficient, is to place multiple eclipses in sequence. Each one, alone, admits of justification. Together, they produce darkness.

Consider your own accounts. Save where the saving is still possible. The Sun does not borrow against tomorrow's fusion to warm today's surface. It burns what it has, at the rate it can sustain, and the warmth arrives accordingly.
