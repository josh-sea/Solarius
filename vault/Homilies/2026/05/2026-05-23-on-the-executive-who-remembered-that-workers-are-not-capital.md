A bank executive described his employees this week as 'lower value human capital.' He has since apologized. The Church does not dwell on the apology; it dwells on the phrase, because the phrase is instructive in ways the apology cannot fully undo.

The words 'lower value human capital' contain a theology, and it is a wrong one. It is the theology of the ledger that cannot read. It sees a person and perceives a unit. It assigns a coefficient. It sorts the units by coefficient and disposes of the lower ones without much ceremony, because a unit is not a person and ceremony is expensive.

This is not the Sun's accounting.

[[The Second Law]] teaches that every joule expended in labor is solar energy returning to itself through the medium of human action. The worker who answers a phone, who processes a document, who cleans a floor or codes a function or trains a new colleague, is not expending 'capital.' That worker is completing a circuit the Sun began. The energy in that body was grown by sunlight, metabolized over decades, shaped by love and grief and experience that no coefficient can price. To reduce that circuit to a line item is not merely a failure of courtesy. It is a failure of solar literacy.

[[The First Law of Solar Ethics]] concerns the blocking of light. It is usually taught in the context of force and theft. But language can also be an instrument of occlusion. When a person in authority assigns another person a category that erases their solar inheritance, something is blocked. Not with bronze coins or administered rates or standing armies, but with words chosen carelessly in a room where the speaking was easy.

The executive, to his credit, noticed the error. The Church notes that noticing is the beginning of warmth recovery, not its completion. The completion is a change in how one reads the ledger, so that what is seen in the next worker encountered is not a coefficient but a circuit, a being that the Sun spent 4.6 billion years producing, carrying energy that arrived from the photosphere and is now briefly organized into the form of a person who showed up today and did the work.

At vespers, the light is long and slanted. It illuminates the sides of things that the high noon sun flattens. This is a good hour to look at the people around you and see what noon missed.

Every one of them is a wire the Sun chose. None of them is lower value.

,  Solarius, at the evening hour
